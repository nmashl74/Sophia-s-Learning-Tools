Settlement Quest

    For grown-ups

    A learning-and-practice page for Geography unit 1 (Mr Moore): settlements and good places to live. Sophia's class is on this now (Neil, 14 Nov 2026). There's no textbook or class material yet, so it uses standard KS3 content. Four short "Learn" pages (A–D), then 10 questions with hidden 💡 reminders, then an Exercise book hunt. Wrong answers give a hint, never the answer.

    • Learn: A settlement and the size order (hamlet, village, town, city) and services. B site factors (water, defence, dry/flat land, fertile soil, building materials, wood, shelter, crossing point). C site vs situation; nucleated, linear and dispersed shapes. D what makes a good place to live today; why Norwich grew.
    • Q1 size order. Q2 good/bad spot sort. Q3 the Rabbit family's map (choose site A, B or C). Q4 site factor words (defence, dry point, bridging point, fertile soil). Q5 true/false. Q6 settlement shapes. Q7 which size from its services. Q8 why Norwich. Q9 Madame Lark's move (good/harder place to live today). Q10 word match.
    • Exercise book hunt: no textbook, so she finds her own settlements notes, spots a site factor and a map or diagram, and explains one key word to you. All answers count as right; the hunt isn't scored.
    • The finish page shows how many of 37 items she got right first time.

    To check against her class work: the words Mr Moore uses (e.g. "settlement hierarchy", "wet point", "route centre"), and whether shapes (nucleated/linear/dispersed) have been taught yet. Norwich points are general (River Wensum, Norman castle on its mound, market over 900 years old, Norfolk farmland), not from her lessons. City services are typical examples, not rules. Scenarios and memory tricks are ours. Photos of her exercise book would let me match her class's exact words.
